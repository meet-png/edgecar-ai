# Battery health against measured capacity — what EVBattery can and cannot show

Generated by `python -m edgecar.bench.battery` from EVBattery (Tsinghua, CC BY 4.0), figshare 23301881, part(s) 3: 176,327 charging snippets from 49 vehicles; 1170 charging sessions with an engineer-measured capacity gained at least 15 % of charge (the rule the vehicle applies to drives). For each, EdgeCar's arithmetic — charge in ÷ state of charge gained — against the label.

**Absolute:** the estimate is 3.77× the label (10th–90th percentile 3.36–4.50×). That is not an error of the method: to anonymise the data the authors "perturbed and interpolated the original average voltage, current, and temperature values" and "randomly shifted and scaled the timestamps" (arXiv:2201.12358, §3), so charge cannot be integrated in real units.

Median correlation **+0.07**. The labels age as expected (they fall with mileage in 7 of 8 vehicles), but the estimate does not follow them: the labels move a median 9 % within a vehicle, while the session-to-session spread of the estimate on this anonymised data is about as large or larger. **This dataset cannot validate the estimate, for or against.**

What follows for the passport: its interval is only the spread of one car's own measurements (on the i3, 23 drives within ±0.2 %), and the report says it is not yet validated against a laboratory capacity test. The next check is TU Munich's *Electric Vehicle Usage and State of Health Data* (mediaTUM 1845276, CC BY-SA 4.0), which records real current and time with measured state of health.
